## What DOSS is
An **AI-native, adaptive ERP / operations cloud** purpose-built for physical-product companies.
DOSS unifies the flow of **goods, dollars, and data** — inventory, procurement, orders,
production, and fulfillment — and connects them to the general ledger **without ripping and
replacing** the existing GL/accounting (e.g. QuickBooks).

Core pieces:
- **Operations modules:** procurement, inventory management, order management.
- **Integrated Data Platform (IDP):** the unifying data layer.
- **Workflow editor:** automate/configure processes without consultants.
- **DataStudio:** dashboards for sales, production, and operational KPIs (no separate BI tool).

## Who it's for (PMF)
Mid-market, **high-growth consumer-product brands** ($10M–$200M revenue, 10–500 employees,
US/Canada) that have outgrown spreadsheets but aren't ready for — or don't want — a heavy legacy
ERP. They typically **outsource production** to co-manufacturers and 3PLs, and are
**"drowning in manual processes"** as SKUs, channels, and order volume scale.

Verticals: food & beverage, beauty/cosmetics, supplements, health & wellness, personal care,
home goods, furniture, pet, apparel, consumer electronics.

## How DOSS wins (vs. alternatives)
- **vs. legacy ERP (NetSuite, Acumatica, Intacct, Dynamics, Epicor):** operations-first instead of
  finance-first; weeks not months to implement; adapts to the business instead of forcing rigid
  templated workflows. DOSS deliberately avoids prospects that *want* a full ERP migration today.
- **vs. QuickBooks + spreadsheets / point tools:** a single operational system of record that
  still sits on top of the existing GL.
- **AI-native** implementation acceleration and operational intelligence as a core differentiator.

## Screening implications for this pipeline
- A great candidate **looks like an emerging CPG brand with a funding/launch/expansion signal**,
  outsourced manufacturing, and a lightweight system stack.
- **Down-rank/avoid:** pure software/services, restaurants/food-service operators, heavy in-house
  manufacturers, anyone already standardized on a full ERP, and the excluded industries.
